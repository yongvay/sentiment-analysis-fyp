Research Questions
RQ1: 
How can an automated multi-model consensus (voting-based) annotation pipeline produce a high-fidelity sentiment-labeled dataset for code-mixed English–Malay Malaysian social media text, and how closely do its labels agree with human annotation?

* Consider 3 models (Voting)
* Try to use LLM instead of transformer model
* Check quality of annotation (compare human and machine)

(https://akademiabaru.com/submit/index.php/ard/article/view/5640/5354) 
(Limitations: The authors noted that the dataset is imbalanced and skewed toward neutral sentiments, which could lead to biased models. They also acknowledged that automated tools occasionally produce "false" labels compared to manual checks)


RQ2: To what extent do multilingual transformer-based models (e.g., XLM-RoBERTa) outperform monolingual baselines in classifying sentiment in highly code-mixed Malaysian (Manglish) social media text?

(https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.987.pdf)
(This paper establishes that popular transformer models like BERT and RoBERTa perform significantly better than monolingual or non-transformer baselines in code-mixed Hinglish and Benglish tasks, especially when fine-tuned with phonetic features.)

One methodological watch-out worth flagging now (it affects whether RQ2 is cleanly answerable): XLM-RoBERTa appears in both phases — as a voter/annotator in RQ1 and as a classifier under evaluation in RQ2. If you train and test RQ2's XLM-RoBERTa on the same auto-labeled data it helped annotate, the result is partly circular (a model will naturally agree with labels a similar model produced). The fix is to make sure RQ2's evaluation runs on the human-validated gold-standard test set, not the machine-labeled portion. Keep that distinction explicit in your methodology and the circularity objection disappears.







RQ3: How can the decision-making process of sentiment detection models be made explainable when processing linguistic features (slang, particles, and syntax) unique to Bahasa Rojak? 
(https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.987.pdf)
(This paper directly applies the SHAP technique to explain how transformer models make decisions on code-mixed text. It extracts the specific terms (regional words and spelling variations) responsible for final predictions, which is exactly what I plan to do for Bahasa Rojak.)
